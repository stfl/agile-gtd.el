#+title: agile-gtd

Sprint planning on top of GTD, in Org mode.

GTD is good at getting everything out of your head into a trusted list. It
says little about choosing from that list once it holds four hundred open
items. Scrum is good at choosing: a ranked backlog, a sprint you commit to,
priorities set on whole pieces of work. It assumes someone grooms the backlog
every week. =agile-gtd= combines the two, so a personal task list sorts itself
the way a well-kept backlog would.

* The idea

You will have far more open tasks than you will ever do, and that is fine. In
any given week only a small part of them matters. The system's job is to show
you that part and keep the rest out of sight without losing any of it.

| From GTD                                          | From Agile                                        |
|---------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| Capture everything into an inbox, clarify later   | One backlog, sorted most important first          |
| Next actions: the physical step you can do now    | A sprint: the slice of the backlog you commit to  |
| Projects: any outcome that needs several steps    | Priority is set on the project, not on each step  |
| Someday/maybe and the tickler for parked work     | Views cut the backlog at a depth you choose       |
| The weekly review catches stuck projects          | Deadlines move work up the backlog as they near   |

* Two ideas Org does not have

*Parent priority.* You prioritise the project, and its next actions follow.
Move a =PROJ= into the sprint and every =NEXT= inside it appears in the sprint
too. No next action gets left behind because nobody moved it into the sprint.
A single step can still be raised above its project when it is more
urgent than the rest; it is never pushed below it.

*Rank.* Every task gets one number, computed from four things: its own
priority, its project's priority, how close its deadline is, and whether a
scheduled date has come due. Views sort by rank and cut at a rank, so filtering
and ordering always agree. A date can only move a task /up/: an unprioritised
task climbs the backlog as its deadline approaches, and a deadline months away
leaves it where its priority put it.

* How you work with it

Priorities run from =A= to =I=. Five views, called ranges, cut the backlog at
increasing depth:

#+begin_example
today  ->  sprint  ->  upcoming  ->  all  ->  someday
 due or    A..C       down to E,    every     plus parked
 overdue              and anything  priority  SOMEDAY work
                      unprioritised
#+end_example

- *Commit to work* by giving its project priority =A=, =B= or =C=. The project's
  next actions now show in the sprint.
- *Leave it on the list* without a priority. It shows in =upcoming=, the
  everyday working view, and rises when its deadline gets close.
- *Stop seeing it every day* by demoting it below =E=. It stays in the backlog
  (=all=) and out of your daily view.
- *Park it* with the =SOMEDAY= tag. It leaves every range but =someday=.
- *Park it until a date* with =SOMEDAY= and a =SCHEDULED= date. That makes it a
  tickler, and it comes back into view on that day.

Inside an open agenda you widen or narrow the range on the spot, so one command
serves both "what do I do now" and "what is on my plate at all".

* What it looks like

A small =todo.org=; only the tax return has a date:

#+begin_src org
,* PROJ [#B] Launch the new website
,** NEXT Write the landing page copy
,** NEXT Pick a hosting provider
,* PROJ Renovate the bathroom
,** NEXT Get three quotes from plumbers
,* NEXT File the tax return
DEADLINE: <2026-09-27 Sun>
,* PROJ [#G] Learn Rust
,** NEXT Read chapter 4 of the book
,* NEXT Try the new ramen place                                    :SOMEDAY:
#+end_src

The Main Agenda (=a=) at the =all= range, captured on 23 September 2026:

#+begin_example
Next Actions [all]

 [#B] Priority B
  NEXT Write the landing page copy
  NEXT Pick a hosting provider
  NEXT File the tax return  due in 4d

 Default Priority
  NEXT Get three quotes from plumbers

 [#G] Priority G
  NEXT Read chapter 4 of the book
#+end_example

- The two website actions carry no priority of their own; they sit at =B=
  because their project does.
- The tax return has no priority either. Its deadline four days out lifts it to
  =B=.
- At =sprint= the list stops after the =[#B]= group. At =upcoming= it stops
  after =Default Priority=, so Rust stays out of the daily view.
- The ramen place appears only at =someday=, under its own =Someday= heading.

* Quickstart

Put your tasks in =todo.org= and =inbox.org= under =org-directory=, then:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(use-package agile-gtd
  :after org
  :config
  (agile-gtd-enable))
#+end_src

Run =M-x org-agenda=, then =a= for the Main Agenda, =i= for the inbox or =rs=
for stuck projects. =agile-gtd-agenda-wider-range= and =agile-gtd-agenda-narrower-range=
change the range of the open agenda; bind them to keys of your choice.

To split work by client or team, register a tag per project:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setopt agile-gtd-projects '((:tag "alpha" :key ?a)
                             (:tag "beta")))
#+end_src

=alpha= then gets its own agenda command, =wa=.

* Install

agile-gtd needs Emacs 30.2 or later; CI runs the tests on Emacs 30.2 and 31.1.
It is not on MELPA. It depends on org-ql, org-super-agenda, org-edna and
org-modern from MELPA, and on [[https://github.com/stfl/org-records-mcp][org-records-mcp]], which is not on MELPA either.
MELPA's =org-mcp= is a different package and does not work here.

With the built-in =package-vc= (Emacs 30):

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(use-package org-records-mcp
  :vc (:url "https://github.com/stfl/org-records-mcp" :branch "main"))

(use-package agile-gtd
  :vc (:url "https://github.com/stfl/agile-gtd")
  :after org
  :config
  (agile-gtd-enable))
#+end_src

With straight, the org-records-mcp recipe is
=(:host github :repo "stfl/org-records-mcp" :branch "main" :files (:defaults "org-records-mcp-stdio.sh"))=.

* Limits

- *It is opinionated and global.* =agile-gtd-enable= sets your TODO keywords,
  priority range, workflow tags, capture templates, agenda files and a set of
  Org options. Agenda commands and capture templates on the keys it uses are
  replaced; others are kept. [[file:docs/configuration.org][What the package configures]] lists every change
  and how to turn parts of it off.
- *It is for one person.* There are no story points, no velocity, no burndown
  and no shared team backlog.
- *It installs no keybindings.* The range commands and chain helpers are
  yours to bind.
- *The deadline table is fixed.* How many days out a deadline counts as =A=,
  =B= and so on is not configurable.
- *org-records-mcp is a hard dependency*, even when you never connect an AI
  assistant.
